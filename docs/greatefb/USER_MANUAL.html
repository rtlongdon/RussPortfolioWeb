GreatEFB User Manual

Complete reference guide — verified against running app and source code

This manual assumes you already know how to read a sectional, fly a heading/track, and interpret standard weather products (METAR/TAF/AIRMET/SIGMET/TFR/NOTAM). It does not re-teach navigation or weather theory — it explains what GreatEFB shows you and how to operate it.

1. System Requirements & Warnings

System Requirements

GreatEFB operates as a moving map and flight planning application on any tablet. For real-time aircraft data (position, altitude, attitude, engine telemetry, and traffic display), a Dynon SkyView avionics system with WiFi connectivity is required. The app functions without SkyView — charts, airspace, flight planning, and pre-downloaded weather layers remain available — but live avionics data will not display nor will traffic or ADSB dependent services (TIS-B/FIS-B products).

Important Safety & Liability Notice

Before using GreatEFB in flight, read and understand all of the following:

  • Pilot Judgment is Final — GreatEFB is a situational awareness tool. The final authority on navigation, weather avoidance, terrain clearance, and airspace compliance rests with the pilot in command and your aircraft's certified instruments. Never make flight decisions based solely on GreatEFB data.
  • Official Weather Briefings Required — Do not substitute GreatEFB weather displays for a full preflight weather briefing from Flight Service (1-800-WX-BRIEF), aviationweather.gov, or NOAA Aviation Weather. Weather on this device may be delayed, incomplete, or inaccurate.
  • Altitude — Use Your Altimeter — GreatEFB displays altitude from your aircraft's altimeter (via Dynon) or tablet GPS. GPS-derived altitude may differ significantly from pressure altitude. Your aircraft's pressure altimeter is the authoritative altitude reference — use it for all altitude-related decisions, including terrain and obstacle clearance.
  • VFR Only — GreatEFB is designed for VFR operations. It is not equipped, certified, or intended for IFR flight, approaches, or operations in instrument meteorological conditions. Use at your own risk.
  • Traffic Display Is Not Collision Avoidance — ADS-B and TIS-B traffic is shown for situational awareness only. It is your responsibility to see and avoid other aircraft. Traffic may be incomplete, delayed, or absent — do not assume ADS-B coverage is continuous or that all aircraft in your vicinity are displayed.
  • NEXRAD for Strategic Planning Only — Weather radar displayed on GreatEFB should inform only your pre-flight and en-route strategic planning, not tactical decisions to avoid active weather. Use on-board radar or real-time PIREP updates for active weather avoidance.
  • Data Accuracy and Currency — All aviation data (charts, airspace, NOTAMs, TFRs, airport information, navaids) comes from government and public sources and is provided without warranty of accuracy, completeness, or timeliness. Before each flight, verify NOTAMs and TFR status through official FAA channels. Outdated data may be dangerous — keep your device's data current.
  • Terrain Data Has Limitations — Terrain layers and elevation data are advisory aids only, not certified terrain awareness systems. Terrain data may be inaccurate or incomplete. Maintain visual scanning and rely on your altimeter for terrain clearance decisions.
  • Tablet Mounting — Position your tablet to avoid obstructing instrument scanning, forward visibility, or access to flight controls.
  • No Warranty — GreatEFB is provided "as is." The developer makes no warranties regarding accuracy, reliability, or fitness for navigation or safety-critical use. The developer is not liable for errors, omissions, or failures in the software or displayed data, nor for any consequences of relying on GreatEFB during flight operations.

2. Introduction

GreatEFB is a moving-map Electronic Flight Bag for tablets, built to work alongside Dynon SkyView avionics in experimental aircraft. It displays your position, track, and altitude over sectional charts with overlays for airspace, airports, traffic, weather, TFRs, and terrain. You can plan flights by creating and editing routes, and the app automatically fetches weather and NOTAMs relevant to your flight plan.

3. Getting Started

No Setup Wizard

The app opens directly to the moving map.

Network

Your tablet must be on the same network as your SkyView system (or whatever device is relaying its UDP data). GreatEFB listens passively on two ports and does not require you to enter an IP address:

  • Port 49155 (ASCII sentences — ADAHRS, system data)
  • Port 8384 (protobuf — GPS, ADAHRS)

Data Source

GreatEFB defaults to Dynon SkyView as its data source (Settings → Data Source). You can switch to your tablet's own GPS instead; doing so will prompt for location permission the first time. The app remembers your last choice and restores it on the next startup.

Confirming Data Flow

As of this build, there is no dedicated "Connected" indicator. The practical ways to confirm data is flowing:

  • Watch the map — your aircraft symbol should appear and move.
  • Open Settings → Debug and enable one of the diagnostic overlays (GPS Debug Display, Dynon Datastreams, or Network (UDP Relay)) to see raw data and packet rates. These are troubleshooting tools, not part of normal flight use, and are off by default.

Charts, Terrain & Airspace Data

Critical: Chart, terrain, and airspace data must be downloaded separately before you fly — see Data Management. This requires your tablet to reach a data server on your local network (discovered automatically via mDNS) — it is not a direct internet download. If you don't already have that server set up on your home network, chart/terrain sync won't find anything to connect to.

4. Moving Map

The map fills the entire screen, displaying all navigational layers, traffic, weather hazards, and your flight plan. The experience is heavily interactive — tapping on features opens context-specific detail dialogs and action menus. A tab bar above the map switches between Map, Flight Plan, and Resume Plan (the last only appears when a flight plan is suspended). A Zulu clock (HH:MM:SSZ) sits next to the tabs.

Floating Controls

Top-right of the map:

  • ⚙ Settings — opens the Settings dialog
  • Center-lock button — toggles between "✋" (free pan) and "⊕" (camera locked to your aircraft, smooth-follow). Panning more than 500m from your aircraft automatically disengages the lock.
  • WX — opens the Weather panel

Data Blocks

TRK, HDG, GS, and ALT are shown as individually draggable, resizable overlays on the map. Their positions are remembered between sessions.

Heading Availability: Magnetic heading (HDG) is only shown when that data is available from SkyView. If using Tablet GPS as your data source, only true track (TRK) and optionally magnetic track (computed from GPS track using magnetic declination) are available — magnetic heading from an attitude system is not available.

Ownship Symbol

A red aircraft icon pointing in the direction of travel, rotating with heading. This represents your own aircraft as designated via Set as Ownship (see below).

Setting Ownship from Traffic

To designate a traffic target as your own aircraft, long-press the traffic target (hold for ~500ms). This process works as follows:

Sanity Check: If the target is implausibly far from your tablet's GPS position, a warning dialog appears: That Doesn't Look Like Your Aircraft. The dialog shows the distance and lets you either cancel or proceed anyway. This check is a soft warning (overridable), not a hard block, since your tablet's GPS fix can be briefly stale or inaccurate.

Confirmation: After passing the sanity check (or if there is no warning), a confirmation dialog asks you to confirm: Set as Ownship?. This warns you that GreatEFB will hide TIS-B traffic reports with that ICAO address from now on.

Aircraft Profile Creation: When you confirm, GreatEFB immediately:

  • Filters out that ICAO address from the traffic display
  • Looks up whether an aircraft profile already exists for that ICAO code (Mode S) or tail number (callsign)
  • If no profile exists: Creates a minimal new profile with just the tail number (or hex ICAO code if no callsign) and the Mode S code populated. You can edit this profile later in Settings → Aircraft → Manage Profiles to add more details (aircraft type, fuel capacity, cruise speed, etc.)
  • If a profile already exists: Uses the existing profile and marks it as Ownship

Changing Ownship: Only one aircraft can be designated as Ownship at a time. If you long-press a different traffic target while ownship is already set, GreatEFB shows a toast message: Ownship already set to {tailNumber}. Change it from Manage Aircraft if needed. To switch to a different aircraft as ownship, go to Settings → Aircraft → Manage Profiles, find the current ownship profile, and uncheck it, then use Manage Profiles to mark a different aircraft as ownship.

Tapping Airports

Tapping an airport opens a detail sheet showing:

  • Position, elevation, and facility type
  • Available frequencies (ATIS, tower, ground, CTAF, etc.)
  • Services and fuel availability
  • Runway dimensions and headings
  • Manager contact information
  • Remarks (e.g., "PPR required", "Turf runway")
  • Current FIS-B METAR/TAF for that airport (if available)
  • Merged NOTAM section (FIS-B plus on-demand ground lookup)
Safety Note: If the NOTAM fetch fails, the app explicitly tells you Unable to check NOTAMs -- do not assume none exist rather than showing a blank result.

Tapping Traffic (ADS-B/TIS-B)

Tapping a traffic target opens a detail dialog showing:

  • Callsign or ICAO address (hex)
  • Altitude (pressure altitude reported by the target)
  • Ground speed and vertical speed
  • Distance and relative bearing from your aircraft
  • Alert status flag (whether in your protected zone)

Tapping Navaids

Tapping a navaid marker (VOR, NDB, etc.) opens a simple dialog showing:

  • Identifier and name
  • Type (VOR, VOR/DME, NDB, etc.)

Tapping PIREPs

Tapping a PIREP symbol opens a detail dialog showing the pilot report's:

  • Type (turbulence, icing, mountain obscuration, wind shear, etc.)
  • Altitude reported
  • Observation time (Zulu)
  • Visibility, temperature, and weather conditions (when reported)
  • Raw text of the full report

Tapping Advisories (AIRMET/SIGMET/G-AIRMET/CWA)

Tapping on an advisory polygon or hazard marking opens a detail dialog showing:

  • Advisory type (AIRMET, SIGMET, G-AIRMET, CWA)
  • Hazard description (e.g., "Moderate Turbulence", "Icing", "IFR Conditions")
  • Valid time window
  • Altitude restrictions (when applicable)
  • Full text of the advisory

Tapping TFRs (Temporary Flight Restrictions)

Tapping a TFR polygon opens a detail dialog showing:

  • TFR type and effective dates/times
  • Altitude restrictions and notam details
  • Linked NOTAM text with full regulatory language
  • Reason for the restriction (airshow, VIP movement, wildfire, etc.)

Multiple Features at One Tap (Stack-Up Dialog)

If you tap on a location where multiple features overlap — for example, an airport that sits inside a TFR zone, or a traffic target near a navaid — GreatEFB shows a selection dialog listing all features at that location. Tap the one you want to view its detail. This prevents accidental openings of the wrong feature when features are stacked closely.

Flight Plan Waypoint Interaction

While a flight plan is active and displayed as a magenta line on the map:

  • Tap a waypoint marker: Opens the detail for that waypoint (airport, navaid, or fix)
  • Long-press a waypoint: (hold for ~500ms) Allows you to delete that waypoint from the active plan
  • Drag a waypoint: Move it to a new location on the map, updating the flight plan geometry in real time

Rubber Band (Waypoint Editing)

While a flight plan is active and displayed on the map, you can edit waypoint positions using the rubber band feature:

  1. Drag a waypoint: Long-press and drag any waypoint marker to a new location on the map
  2. Temporary yellow line: As you drag, a yellow line appears connecting the waypoint's original position to your current drag position, showing where the waypoint will move to
  3. Accept or cancel:
    • Release your drag to accept the new waypoint position — the flight plan updates and the yellow line disappears
    • Tap elsewhere or press back to cancel the move — the waypoint snaps back to its original position and the yellow line disappears

This allows you to fine-tune your route on the fly without opening the Flight Plan tab — useful for avoiding weather, terrain, or airspace as conditions change during flight.

5. Weather

The WX button opens a menu with seven modes, each showing a real-time synthesis of two independent data sources: FIS-B (received over the air from Dynon via port 8384) and ground fetch (Wi-Fi HTTP download from aviationweather.gov and other public endpoints). Understanding how these two sources work together and when they disagree is key to trusting GreatEFB's weather picture.

How FIS-B Works

FIS-B (Flight Information Service - Broadcast) is a UAT datalink that Dynon SkyView broadcasts continuously on port 8384. GreatEFB receives and decodes this stream passively without making any network requests. FIS-B updates arrive roughly every 10 minutes for each product type (METAR, TAF, AIRMET, etc.), and coverage is typically strong within 100 nm of any ground station — but it has natural dead zones and can fade in remote areas or near terrain.

Key point: FIS-B is "push" — the broadcast reaches you without any action needed, but you only get what the nearest ground station is currently broadcasting. That set changes slowly (10-minute update cycles) and covers a fixed regional area, not your specific location.

How Ground Fetch Works

When your tablet has Wi-Fi connectivity, GreatEFB automatically fetches the latest METAR, TAF, AIRMET/SIGMET, Winds Aloft, and NOTAMs from public aviation weather endpoints (primarily aviationweather.gov). A background worker runs this fetch every 15 minutes, filtering results to your current position (200 nm radius) and any active flight plan (25 nm corridor either side of course). This data is always current and nationwide, but requires Wi-Fi — if you lose connectivity, ground fetches stop.

Filtering: Ground fetch automatically excludes distant stations. A station 300 nm away won't appear, even if FIS-B happens to be broadcasting it. This keeps the list manageable and relevant to your flight.

Status Indicator (Green = OK, Red = Failing)

Each weather mode displays a status line showing the last ground-fetch outcome:

  • Green — The most recent ground fetch succeeded. You're seeing current data from aviationweather.gov.
  • Red — The ground fetch is currently failing or has not yet succeeded. You may still see FIS-B data if you're in range, but ground-sourced (and thus nationwide-sourced) products are unavailable.

Retry & Timing Model

When a ground fetch succeeds, GreatEFB waits 15 minutes until the next normal update. If a fetch fails (network error, endpoint down, no GPS fix yet), GreatEFB automatically retries after 30 seconds, then again if that fails, repeating this short retry cycle until the fetch succeeds. This means:

  • A temporary network glitch triggers a re-fetch within 30 seconds, not a 15-minute wait.
  • Repeated failures (e.g., Wi-Fi down, unreachable endpoint) keep retrying every 30 seconds indefinitely, so as soon as connectivity returns, data loads.
  • Successful fetches reset the timer to the normal 15-minute periodic schedule.

What Happens When Sources Disagree

It's normal for FIS-B and ground fetch to show slightly different data for the same station. FIS-B may be broadcasting an older observation (10+ minutes behind), while ground fetch pulls the very latest. In practice:

  • METAR: ground fetch is nearly always newer. Ground data replaces FIS-B if both are present for the same station.
  • TAF: less frequent updates; both sources often show the same forecast.
  • AIRMET/SIGMET: both show active hazards, but the boundary polygons may differ slightly between FIS-B and ground sources.

Graphical AIRMETs (G-AIRMETs)

G-AIRMETs are forecasted graphical depictions of hazards (turbulence, icing, mountain obscuration) with explicit time-range validity and altitude restrictions. Unlike text AIRMETs (which are issued on a fixed schedule), G-AIRMETs are issued continuously and show polygon-shaped hazard areas with precise boundaries.

When viewing the Advisories mode in the Weather panel, you see both text AIRMETs (for classic convective hazards, wind shear, etc.) and G-AIRMET polygons (for moderate/severe turbulence, icing, and mountain obscuration). Each G-AIRMET hazard is colored by type — turbulence in one color, icing in another — and tap a polygon to see the detailed forecast text and altitude restrictions.

Center Weather Advisories (CWAs)

Center Weather Advisories are issued by Air Route Traffic Control Centers (ARTCCs) to alert pilots of significant weather within their airspace. Unlike SIGMETs (which cover broader regions and are national in scope), CWAs are issued on a center-by-center basis and tend to be more localized and timely for IFR operations.

CWAs typically cover:

  • Moderate to severe turbulence
  • Moderate to severe icing
  • Wind shear hazards
  • Rapidly developing weather (thunderstorms, frontal passages)

In GreatEFB, CWAs appear in the Advisories panel alongside AIRMETs and SIGMETs. They're shown as text advisories with their issuing ARTCC center name and time range. Tap a CWA to see the full advisory text and any polygon geometry (if available).

Staleness & Expiry

Weather products age out based on their own validity windows or a project-wide staleness threshold (roughly 90 minutes for METAR, longer for TAF). Once stale, an entry is removed from the list. This prevents you from seeing old data without realizing it's obsolete.

When You Have No GPS Fix

If your aircraft position is unknown (no GPS lock yet), GreatEFB uses the last-known position (persisted on disk) to filter ground fetches. If there's no last-known position either (first ever run), a ground fetch is skipped entirely this cycle and re-attempted after 30 seconds — once a real GPS fix arrives, filtering works normally.

Per-Airport Weather & NOTAMs

Beyond the WX panel, you can tap any airport on the map to see its weather and NOTAMs directly without opening the full weather modes. This uses the same combined FIS-B + ground fetch logic, but scoped to just that airport.

Colored Dot System (Flight Category Visualization)

Each airport on the map displays a colored dot reflecting the latest METAR's flight category — a quick visual scan of your route's weather at a glance. The dots scale with zoom level, making them larger as you zoom in for easier reading. Each color represents a decision point:

Dot Color Flight Category Ceiling Condition Visibility Condition Meaning
Bright Green VFR ≥ 3,000 ft ≥ 5 SM Visual flight minimums met
Bright Blue MVFR 1,000–3,000 ft 3–5 SM Marginal VFR
Bright Red LIFR < 1,000 ft < 3 SM Below VFR minimums
Gray — Unknown Unknown No current METAR or report has gone stale; airport not drawn on map but remains tappable
About the 3-Tier System: GreatEFB uses VFR/MVFR/LIFR, collapsing the traditional IFR tier into LIFR. This simplifies decision-making: red means "below VFR minimums, requires instrument rating," full stop. The logic is straightforward: if either ceiling is below 1,000 ft or visibility is below 3 SM, the airport is red.

How Categories Are Derived

The color is computed from the latest METAR's ceiling (lowest BKN/OVC layer) and prevailing visibility. The parsing is deliberately approximate — meant as a situational-awareness aid for scanning your route, not a substitute for reading the actual verbatim METAR text (which the WX panel shows in full). If a METAR is missing ceiling or visibility data entirely, that airport won't show a dot at all (no color assigned means no map-visual advantage over the underlying sectional).

When Dots Disappear

Airports without a current, fresh METAR don't display a dot — even though they remain tappable. This keeps the map clean and prevents gray dots from duplicating the airport symbols already shown on your sectional chart. A tap on a non-reporting airport is still possible (the airport itself is still visible on the chart), and the detail view will show "No METAR" rather than guessing with an outdated report.

Safety Note: When a NOTAM lookup fails for a specific airport, the app shows Unable to check NOTAMs -- do not assume none exist. This is intentional — an unknown NOTAM status is never the same as "no NOTAMs." Always cross-check with an official briefing before flight.

6. Terrain

The Terrain Heatmap (Settings → Layers) overlays pre-baked elevation data, dynamically recolored in real time based on your aircraft's altitude. It uses a single tile set with runtime recoloring (no altitude buckets) and LRU caching for performance.

Color Classification

The heatmap displays three colors based on terrain elevation relative to your altitude:

Color Terrain Elevation Relative to Aircraft Meaning
Red Within 500 ft above or below Danger zone — collision risk if not cleared
Yellow 500–1000 ft below aircraft Caution zone — terrain is rising toward you
Transparent More than 1000 ft below aircraft Safe — adequate clearance from terrain

The transparency allows the base sectional chart to show through, so you can cross-check terrain hazards against labeled geography and airports.

Data Source & Tile Format

Terrain data comes from USGS 3DEP (3D Elevation Program) at 30-meter resolution, pre-processed into Web Mercator PNG tiles at zoom levels 8–11 (regional overview to detailed approach zones). Each tile is 256×256 pixels, with elevation encoded in the RED channel of grayscale image data:

  • Encoding: Pixel value × (15,000 ft ÷ 255) = terrain elevation
  • Range: 0–15,000 ft (covers CONUS, including Mt. Whitney at 14,505 ft)
  • Format: PNG grayscale, single RED channel per pixel

Tiles are generated once at build time (not on-device), so runtime performance is unconstrained — the app can recolor a full 3×3 viewport (9 tiles) in roughly 50 ms.

Runtime Recoloring (How It Works)

When you climb or descend, GreatEFB does not re-download tiles. Instead, it recolors the existing tiles based on your current altitude:

  1. Altitude change detected: If your altitude changes by more than 100 ft (roughly the resolution of a single tile), the recoloring pipeline triggers.
  2. Clear cache: The 9-tile LRU cache is flushed to free memory.
  3. Recolor on-demand: As you pan or zoom the map, each tile is loaded, re-evaluated against your new altitude, and recolored with the current red/yellow/transparent classification.
  4. Layer swap: To avoid flicker, GreatEFB alternates between two layer pairs — one layer fades in while the other fades out — so the transition is smooth even as you move around the map.

Why this architecture? Pre-baking tiles (no device recomputation) keeps the app responsive. Runtime recoloring (not altitude-bucketed tiles) means your altitude thresholds are always current, and GreatEFB uses minimal storage — 7 regions × 9 zoom levels = roughly 5,000 tiles total at ~1.5 GB, not 10× that for multiple altitude buckets.

Update Throttle & Caching

The heatmap uses a 1-second / 100-ft throttle to avoid constant unnecessary recoloring:

  • Changes smaller than 100 ft don't trigger an update (normal cruise descent/climb is handled smoothly without flickering)
  • Multiple altitude changes within 1 second are batched into a single recolor cycle
  • The LRU cache holds 9 tiles (a 3×3 map viewport), reused as you scroll without re-fetching from disk

This keeps recoloring work and tile I/O minimal during normal flight.

Ground Suppression

The terrain heatmap is automatically hidden when your groundspeed drops below 25 knots (taxiing, parked, or standing). At these speeds, terrain warnings are meaningless and only clutter the map. When you take off (groundspeed >25 kt), the heatmap resumes without requiring any action from you. This is separate from your user preference to toggle the layer on/off — landing doesn't reset your Settings.

Airport Exclusion Zones

GreatEFB suppresses terrain alerts within 2 nm and 500 ft of an airport's field elevation. This prevents false red-zone warnings from terrain slopes adjacent to the runway or natural terrain rises within the traffic pattern, where being several hundred feet above field elevation is normal and expected during approach/climb-out. The exclusion zone is computed per-airport in real time during recoloring, so overlapping zones around nearby fields correctly combine.

Opacity Control

A Terrain Opacity slider (Settings → Layers) controls the heatmap's transparency:

  • 20%: Very subtle overlay; easy to see the chart beneath, harder to see terrain urgency
  • 60% (default): Good balance — terrain hazards stand out without obscuring the chart
  • 100%: Maximum opacity; terrain fills the display but chart detail is hard to read

If you're in areas with rugged terrain, higher opacity makes hazards impossible to miss. In flatter terrain, lower opacity keeps the map less cluttered.

Troubleshooting Terrain Issues

Heatmap not showing

  • Confirm Settings → Layers → Terrain Heatmap is checked (enabled)
  • Confirm your groundspeed is >25 kt (the heatmap is auto-suppressed on the ground)
  • Confirm elevation tiles for your region have been downloaded via Data Management (terrain will show Not cached until synced)

Terrain appears garbled or colored strangely

  • This is extremely rare and typically indicates corrupted tile files
  • Try re-syncing your region via Data Management
Planned, not yet available: a side-view terrain/airspace corridor profile (similar to a Garmin VSD) is in design but not built. Today, terrain awareness is 2D-only (the heatmap above) — there is no vertical profile view.

7. Traffic

The Traffic (ADS-B/TIS-B) layer (on by default) displays nearby aircraft on the map, received from your Dynon SkyView avionics. Each target is shown as a colored triangle pointing in the aircraft's direction of travel, with a callsign or ICAO address and relative altitude label.

Data Source: GDL90 Traffic Reports

Traffic targets come from GDL90 Type 0x14 messages broadcast by SkyView at roughly 1 Hz per aircraft. These messages contain:

  • ADS-B: Aircraft with functioning transponders broadcasting their own position and altitude (any Mode S aircraft within range)
  • TIS-B: Secondary surveillance radar targets provided by ground stations, filling in gaps for aircraft without ADS-B

SkyView merges both sources into a single traffic stream, so GreatEFB sees a unified view. Coverage depends on your altitude, terrain, and proximity to radar/ADS-B infrastructure — typical range is 10–50 nm depending on aircraft altitude and antenna quality.

Target Display & Labels

Each traffic target appears as a small triangle on the map. The triangle points in the direction the aircraft is traveling (track angle), and its color indicates status:

Triangle Color Meaning
Blue Normal traffic — no proximity alert
Red Proximity alert — SkyView has flagged this target as being projected to enter your aircraft's protected zone (based on closure rate, time to closest approach, and altitude). GreatEFB is simply displaying SkyView's threat assessment; the threat volume calculation is done by your avionics, not by the app.

Above each triangle, you see a label with:

  • Callsign or ICAO address: Callsign if available (e.g., "N12345"), or a 6-digit hex ICAO address (e.g., "A1B2C3") if no callsign is transmitted
  • Relative altitude: A signed value in hundreds of feet, relative to your aircraft (e.g., "+02" = 200 ft above, "–03" = 300 ft below). This uses pressure altitude for consistency with transponder altimetry
  • Trend arrow: A small triangle (▲ = climbing, ▼ = descending) if the target is moving vertically more than ~100 fpm (vertical speed); no arrow if level

Staleness & Auto-Removal

Once a target is received, it stays on the map for 15 seconds without a fresh report. If SkyView doesn't hear from an aircraft for 15 seconds (out of range, landed, or communication lost), that target disappears from the map automatically. This keeps the display clutter-free and ensures you're not seeing stale, potentially incorrect positions.

Self-Filtering & Ownship Echo

When your own aircraft broadcasts via ADS-B, Dynon's ground station receiver may bounce your own transponder signal back to you as a TIS-B echo. To keep your own aircraft off the traffic display:

  1. Automatic filtering (initial): When you first start GreatEFB with no aircraft profiles saved, it uses a GPS-proximity heuristic: if a target is within ~1 nm of your tablet's own GPS position, it's assumed to be your own echo and filtered out.
  2. Explicit Mode S assignment (recommended): Once you create an aircraft profile in Settings → Aircraft → Manage Profiles, enter that aircraft's Mode S code (transponder ICAO address, a 6-digit hex number). GreatEFB will then filter by exact ICAO address match instead of proximity, which is more reliable.
  3. Long-press override: If you see your own echo on the map and want to explicitly mark it as Ownship, long-press the target for 500 ms — a dialog offers Set as Ownship?. GreatEFB will remember this ICAO address and filter it out on future sessions.

Only one aircraft can be marked as Ownship at a time. If you try to set a second, GreatEFB will show Only One Ownship Allowed.

Tapping a Target for Details

Tap any target (not a long-press) to open a detail dialog showing:

  • Callsign: The aircraft's identifier
  • ICAO address: The Mode S/6-digit hex code
  • Altitude: Pressure altitude reported by the target (in feet)
  • Ground speed: Velocity across the ground (knots)
  • Vertical speed: Climb/descent rate (feet per minute)
  • Distance: Slant range from your aircraft (nautical miles)
  • Relative bearing: Compass direction from your aircraft (e.g., "traffic at your 2 o'clock")
  • Alert flag: Whether this target triggered a collision alert on your avionics

Integration with Avionics

GreatEFB's traffic display mirrors what your SkyView avionics are already showing — it's a map-based visualization of the same GDL90 data stream your panel is using. The alert status (red triangle) comes directly from SkyView's own alert logic, so if SkyView has flagged a target as a threat, GreatEFB shows it in red. Conversely, if you're seeing something on the map that doesn't match SkyView's display, it may be a stale or anomalous report that hasn't been received recently.

Limitations

  • Coverage gaps: VFR altitudes, remote areas, and terrain-blocked regions may have no traffic visible even if aircraft are present
  • Display time lag: Reports arrive roughly 1 Hz per aircraft; position updates are delayed by SkyView's own processing (typically under 5 seconds)
  • Relative altitude accuracy: Based on transponder-reported pressure altitude, not GPS altitude — in non-standard atmospheric conditions, relative altitude may differ from true geometric separation

8. Flight Planning

The Flight Plan tab is a full-featured waypoint editor for building and navigating routes. It displays your planned route on the map as a magenta line with course/distance labels, and lets you define performance parameters for each leg.

Adding Waypoints

Tap + Add Waypoint to search for and add a waypoint. The search accepts:

  • Airport identifiers: KJFK, PDX, SFO (with or without leading "K")
  • City names: "Seattle" returns all airports in Seattle (KBFI, KSEA, KRNT, etc.); search works across all states in your loaded database
  • Navaids: SEA, OAK, SJC
  • Fixes: SHAKO, FLAPT, LEAPT

Search is case-insensitive and looks across all loaded airport, navaid, and fix data. As you type, matching results appear, showing the identifier, city, and state for airports. Tap a result to add it as the next waypoint in your plan.

Waypoint Management

Once added, waypoints appear in order from top (departure) to bottom (destination). For each waypoint, you can:

  • Move up/down: Reorder legs in your route
  • Delete: Remove a waypoint (requires confirmation)
  • Edit leg parameters: For each leg (segment between two waypoints), edit:
    • Ground Speed (knots): Your expected GS for ETE/ETA calculations
    • Altitude (feet): Planned cruise altitude for this leg
    • Fuel Burn (gallons): Expected fuel consumption rate

Route Display on the Map

Your flight plan appears on the map as:

  • Magenta line: The route connecting all waypoints in order
  • Waypoint markers: Small symbols at each waypoint location
  • Course/distance labels: A box along each leg showing:
    • Magnetic course (e.g., "045°M")
    • Distance (e.g., "47 nm")
    These labels automatically rotate to align with each leg as you pan/zoom the map.

Computed Leg Information

For each leg, GreatEFB automatically computes:

  • True course: Calculated from waypoint coordinates
  • Magnetic course: Converted using magnetic declination (WMM model)
  • Distance: Great-circle distance in nautical miles
  • ETE (Estimated Time Enroute): Based on your entered ground speed
  • ETA (Estimated Time of Arrival): Zulu time at destination, calculated from flight start time

You can edit ground speed, altitude, and fuel burn per leg — these are your inputs for preflight planning and used by GreatEFB for ETA calculations.

Plan Operations

Reverse Flight Plan: Reverses the entire route (useful for return flights). Waypoints swap order, and all calculations update immediately.

Save Flight Plan: Saves your current route to the device's local database. Requires at least 2 waypoints. A dialog prompts for a name; GreatEFB suggests an auto-generated name based on departure and destination (e.g., "BLI_PDX_1"). You can edit this name or accept the suggestion. Once saved, the plan persists even after the app restarts.

Load Flight Plan: Opens a list of previously saved plans. Tap a plan to load it, replacing your current working plan. Each saved plan shows its waypoint sequence; you can also delete a saved plan directly from this list (with confirmation).

Clear Plan: Erases all waypoints from the current plan. This clears your work in the Flight Plan tab but does not delete any saved plans.

Route-Based Data Fetching & Route Conditions

When you modify your flight plan (add/remove/reorder waypoints), GreatEFB automatically refreshes route-based weather, PIREP, and advisory data. This means:

  • Weather (METAR, TAF, wind aloft) is re-fetched for stations along your new route (not just your current position)
  • NOTAMs and advisories are checked for your planned corridor (25 nm either side of course)
  • PIREPs and G-AIRMETs are filtered to your flight path

This happens automatically without you needing to manually refresh — just edit your flight plan and the relevant data updates immediately.

Route Conditions Button & Display

The Route Conditions button in the Flight Plan tab opens a summary view of all weather and hazard data along your planned route. This complements the moving map's graphical layers by showing text-based products in one place.

What's Displayed:

  • METAR: Observations from airports along your route, color-coded by flight category:
    • Green = VFR (ceiling ≥3000 ft, visibility ≥5 SM)
    • Blue = MVFR (ceiling 1000–3000 ft, visibility 3–5 SM)
    • Red = LIFR (ceiling <1000 ft, visibility <3 SM)
  • TAF: Terminal area forecasts for route airports
  • PIREP: Pilot reports of turbulence, icing, sky conditions, and urgency flags along your route corridor
  • NOTAM: Notices to airmen, scoped to your route's airport waypoints only (departure, destination, and via airports) — not the entire route corridor, since corridor-wide NOTAMs would include irrelevant point obstructions along cruise altitudes
  • Footer Note: TFR, AIRMET/SIGMET, G-AIRMET, and CWA hazards are displayed graphically on the map and are not repeated in this text summary

All stations and reports are sorted by their position along your route, not by raw distance to it — so a station near the destination appears in the order you'll encounter it, not just because it happens to sit closer to the course line.

Caching Strategy

Route Conditions uses a shared 5-minute cooldown to prevent redundant network requests:

  • Trigger 1 — Dialog Opens: When you open Route Conditions, it fetches fresh data from 5 network endpoints (METAR/TAF, PIREP, G-AIRMET, CWA, NOTAM)
  • Trigger 2 — Flight Plan Changes: If you edit your flight plan while the dialog is open, a debounced refresh (100ms delay) re-fetches for the new route
  • Shared Cooldown: Both triggers share a 5-minute cooldown keyed by route identity (waypoint identifiers). If you switch between the Map tab and Route Conditions within 5 minutes of the same route, the second trigger skips the fetch to avoid duplicate work. Different routes bypass the cooldown immediately
  • Why Shared? MapFragment also triggers a route-corridor refresh when you return from another tab. Without a shared cooldown, returning to the map after viewing Route Conditions would immediately re-fetch the same 5 endpoints again, defeating the point of the 5-minute window

Suspending a Flight Plan (Direct-To)

While flying a flight plan, you can use the direct-to function to navigate directly to any point on the map. To do this:

  1. Tap on any waypoint, airport, or other navigable point on the map
  2. In the detail box that appears, select the "Direct To" option

When you select Direct To with an active flight plan loaded:

  • Your current flight plan is automatically suspended and saved
  • A new direct-to plan is created with just that single destination
  • The Resume Plan tab appears on the map, allowing you to restore your original plan when ready

This workflow lets you temporarily break off from your original route for a diversion or other needs, then easily resume your planned route once the diversion is complete.

Resume Plan Tab

The Resume Plan tab appears only when you have a suspended flight plan. Selecting it shows a Suspended Flight Plan dialog with three options:

  • Resume: Restore your original plan and clear the direct-to plan
  • Discard: Permanently delete the suspended plan and clear the tab
  • Cancel: Return to the map without changing anything

After choosing an action, you're returned to the Map tab. If no plan is currently suspended, this tab doesn't appear at all.

Limitations & Notes

  • Minimum waypoints: A plan must have at least 2 waypoints to save (departure and destination)
  • Search scope: Waypoint search is limited to data that has been downloaded via Data Management. If a navaid or fix doesn't appear in search, it may not be in your local database.
  • Persistent storage: Flight plans are stored in GreatEFB's local app database, not synced to the cloud. Exporting/importing routes is not yet supported.

9. Aircraft Profiles

Settings → Aircraft → Manage Profiles opens Aircraft Profiles, a full-screen list of saved aircraft. Each profile stores comprehensive data about one aircraft, used for ownship identification, flight planning, and preflight documentation.

Profile List View

The main screen shows all saved aircraft grouped by tail number. Each entry displays:

  • Tail number (e.g., "N12345")
  • Aircraft type (e.g., "Cessna 172")
  • PIC (Pilot in Command) name
  • Cruise speed (knots)
  • Usable fuel (gallons)
  • Ownship badge (appears if this is your designated aircraft for traffic filtering)

Adding a New Aircraft

Tap + Add Aircraft to open the profile editor. You can populate as much or as little as you want — GreatEFB requires only the tail number:

  • Tail Number: Your aircraft's registration (e.g., "N12345")
  • Aircraft Type: Model name (e.g., "Cessna 172")
  • PIC Name: Pilot in Command (your name)
  • PIC Contact Info: Phone/email for emergencies
  • Home Base: Your primary airport
  • Mode S Code: Your aircraft's transponder ICAO address (6-digit hex, e.g., "A1B2C3"). Used to auto-identify your aircraft in the traffic display and filter out your own TIS-B echo.
  • Cruise Speed: Expected cruise airspeed in knots (used for ETE/ETA calculations in flight planning)
  • Usable Fuel: Gallons (used for flight planning fuel calculations)
  • Empty Weight: Aircraft empty weight (pounds)
  • Max Gross Weight: Maximum gross weight (pounds)
  • Fuel Endurance: Hours (burn rate derived from fuel and endurance)
  • Sink Rate: Best glide descent rate (feet per minute)
  • Wake Turbulence Category: Small / Large / Heavy (for flight plan filing)
  • Equipment Code: ICAO equipment designation. See ICAO 5-letter format below; used when filing flight plans or NOTAMs requiring your avionics capability string.
  • Surveillance Code: Transponder mode (Mode A/C, Mode S, ADS-B, etc.)
  • Other Notes: Any additional aircraft-specific information
  • Aircraft Icon: Visual representation on the map (plane, helicopter, etc.)

ICAO Equipment Codes

The Equipment Code field uses a 5-character ICAO string (e.g., "SDE2E") to describe your avionics suite. This is used when filing flight plans or NOTAMs. Common codes include:

  • D: DME (distance measuring equipment)
  • E: Mode C transponder with altitude reporting
  • S: Mode S transponder
  • L: ILS (instrument landing system) approach capable
  • A: VOR (navigational aid) equipped
  • G: GNSS (GPS) equipped
  • /: No transponder / basic equipment

Consult your aircraft documentation or an AFD (Airport Facility Directory) entry for your aircraft's proper equipment code. This field is optional but recommended for completeness.

Managing Profiles

Edit: Tap a profile to open the editor and modify any field.

Mark as Ownship: Each profile has an Ownship toggle. Only one aircraft can be marked ownship at a time. When enabled:

  • That aircraft's Mode S code (if present) is used to filter out your TIS-B echo from the traffic display
  • The profile shows an Ownship badge in the profile list
  • You can change ownship by toggling a different profile's ownship flag (the prior one is automatically unchecked)

Rename: Tap the rename icon to change the profile's display name (separate from the tail number stored in the profile).

Delete: Tap the delete icon to remove a profile. A confirmation dialog appears before deletion.

Ownship Assignment via Traffic

You can also set ownship directly from the moving map by long-pressing a traffic target (see Moving Map → Ownship Symbol). If no profile exists for that aircraft's ICAO code or tail number, GreatEFB creates a minimal profile automatically. You can then edit that profile here to add more details.

10. Data Management

Settings → Downloads → Download Navigator opens the Regional Data Manager, a grid-based interface for managing charts, terrain, and navigation data. All data is synced from a local network server (not the internet), so GreatEFB works completely offline once downloaded.

How the Data Server Works

GreatEFB requires a companion data server running on your home network. This server is a Flask HTTP application (included in the GreatEFB distribution under data_generation/Flask_update_server) that:

  • Hosts pre-built regional data files (charts as MBTiles, elevation tiles, airports/navaids databases, airspace polygons)
  • Advertises itself on the network using mDNS (so your tablet finds it without a hardcoded IP)
  • Serves a JSON manifest for each region/data-type combination (list of files + timestamps)
  • Allows tablets to download only the files that have changed since the last sync
Prerequisites: Before you can download any data, you must have:
  • The Flask data server running on your home network (on a PC, Mac, or Linux machine)
  • Your tablet on the same Wi-Fi network
If you don't have the server set up yet, see the server's own README in data_generation/Flask_update_server for setup instructions.

Server Discovery (mDNS)

When you open the Data Manager, GreatEFB automatically searches for the data server via mDNS (multicast DNS) — a zero-configuration protocol that lets devices on the same network find each other by name rather than IP address. The server advertises itself as _greatefb._tcp, and GreatEFB's discovery looks for that service.

Why mDNS? Home Wi-Fi routers hand out new IP addresses on every DHCP lease renewal. Rather than requiring you to enter the server's current IP (and re-enter it every time DHCP changes), mDNS lets the tablet find the server by name — it "just works" as long as the server is running and on the same network.

A Check Server button forces an immediate re-discovery. This is useful if you just started the server or switched networks.

Status Indicator

A status line at the top shows:

  • Server: OK (green) — mDNS discovery succeeded and the server is reachable. You can proceed with syncing.
  • Server: Unreachable (red) — The tablet cannot find the server via mDNS. Confirm the server is running and both devices are on the same Wi-Fi network, then tap Check Server to retry.

The Sync Grid

Below the status line is a grid of checkboxes, one for each combination of:

  • 6 data types: elevation (terrain tiles), charts (sectional/TAC/flyway MBTiles), airports, navaids, fixes, airspace
  • 7 regions: NW, SW, NC, EC, NE, SC, SE (FAA Chart Supplement aligned)

Each cell's color and label tell you the cache status:

Cell Status Color Meaning
Not cached Gray Not yet downloaded. Syncing this cell will fetch all files for that region/type.
Up to date Green Downloaded and matches the server's latest version. Syncing will skip it (no re-download needed).
Out of date Orange Downloaded but the server has a newer version. Syncing will re-download changed files only.
Unknown Blue-gray Server is unreachable or was unreachable when last checked. Status cannot be determined.

How Sync Works (Manifest-Based Deduplication)

When you tap GO to sync, GreatEFB does not blindly re-download everything. Instead:

  1. Fetch the manifest: For each selected region/data-type, download a JSON manifest file listing all files, their sizes, and timestamps.
  2. Compare timestamps: For each file in the manifest, check if a local copy exists and whether the local file is older than the server version.
  3. Download only changed files: Download only files that don't exist locally or are older than the server version. Unchanged files are skipped entirely.
  4. Update cache status: Once all files for a region/type are successfully downloaded, mark that cell as Up to date and remember the server's timestamp.

This "delta sync" approach means re-syncing an already-current region is fast — only truly new or modified files transfer.

Partial Sync Safety: If a sync is interrupted mid-region (e.g., connection drop), GreatEFB does NOT mark that region as up-to-date. The next sync attempt will re-check and re-download any missing files, so you won't be left with a silently-incomplete dataset.

Storage & Persistence

Downloaded data is stored in GreatEFB's app-private storage on your tablet (typically under /data/data/com.oddharmonic.greategfb or similar, depending on device):

  • Charts: MBTiles files (one per chart, 50–90 MB each) stored flat in charts_mbtiles/
  • Elevation: Terrain tiles (PNG, RED-channel encoded) in elevation_tiles_prebaked/
  • Airports/Navaids/Fixes: SQLite or JSON databases, organized by region in airports_data/, etc.
  • Airspace: GeoJSON polygons, organized by region in airspace_data/

All files are cached persistently — if you kill and restart the app, the data is still there and ready to use, even without network connectivity.

Sync Selection & Progress

  • Check the cells you want to sync
  • The "Selected: N items" counter shows how many region/type combinations you've picked
  • Tap GO to start the sync; a progress bar shows which file is downloading and overall progress
  • Tap Clear to deselect everything at once

Syncs typically complete in minutes for a single region (depending on data size and network speed). Large regions like SW (Arizona, California, Nevada, Utah, Colorado, New Mexico) with all data types can take longer.

Troubleshooting Sync Failures

Server: Unreachable

  • Confirm the data server is running on your PC/Mac/Linux machine
  • Confirm both your tablet and server are on the same Wi-Fi network
  • Tap Check Server to retry discovery
  • If problems persist, check the server's own logs (see its README)

Sync hangs or downloads very slowly

  • Chart files (50–90 MB) can take 1–2 minutes each on slower Wi-Fi (5 Mbps or less)
  • If a download gets stuck, the app will time out and retry — this is normal for large files on congested networks
  • If repeated retries fail, check your Wi-Fi signal strength and router logs

Cell shows "Unknown" after server is running

  • The status was cached the last time the server was unreachable
  • Tap Check Server to refresh the cache

11. Settings

The Settings dialog (⚙ on the map) controls map display, data sources, and app behavior. All settings persist across restarts.

Data Source

Choose where GreatEFB gets your position and attitude data:

  • Dynon SkyView (default) — Uses UDP data from your avionics on ports 49155 and 8384. Requires your tablet to be on the same network as SkyView.
  • Tablet GPS — Uses your tablet's built-in GPS receiver instead. First use will prompt for location permission. Provides position only; altitude and heading come from the tablet's sensors.

The app remembers your last choice and restores it on restart.

Layers

Toggle individual map overlays on and off:

  • Class B/C/D/E Airspace: Vector airspace outlines showing controlled airspace boundaries. Class E is off by default (less cluttered for VFR operations).
  • Airport Identifiers: Text labels showing airport codes (off by default to reduce map clutter; tap an airport to see its full information).
  • Chart Layer: The base sectional/TAC chart (on by default; required for visual reference).
  • Navaids: VOR, NDB, and other radio navigation aids (off by default).
  • Fixes: Named waypoint fixes (off by default).
  • Traffic (ADS-B/TIS-B): Nearby aircraft (on by default).
  • NEXRAD Radar: Weather radar overlay (requires either regional radar data to be downloaded OR FIS-B data available).
  • TFRs: Temporary flight restrictions shown as polygons on the map (on by default).
  • Terrain Heatmap: Elevation data with runtime recoloring. Includes an opacity slider (20–100%) to balance hazard visibility against chart readability. Automatically suppressed when groundspeed drops below 25 knots (taxiing/parked).

Airport Types

Filter which airport classes appear on the map:

  • Aerodrome: Conventional airports (on by default).
  • Heliport: Helicopter landing zones (on by default).
  • Seaplane Base: Water landing areas (off by default).
  • Ultralight: Ultralight strips (off by default).

Debug

Advanced diagnostic tools, not for normal flight use. These are disabled by default.

  • GPS Debug Display: Shows your tablet's raw GPS position, accuracy, and fix quality as an overlay on the map.
  • Dynon Datastreams: Real-time display of parsed ADAHRS (attitude/heading), GPS, and system data as received from SkyView.
  • Network (UDP Relay): Shows whether this app instance is the relay (PRIMARY, receiving real UDP traffic) or a follower, plus packet rates per port. Useful for troubleshooting network connectivity.
  • Stop Ground Updates (FIS-B Playback Testing): Halts the background weather fetch worker, useful for replaying captured FIS-B data without interference from live ground updates.
  • Clear Weather + TFR Data (Debug): Wipes all cached weather and TFR data from memory and storage. Used for testing data restoration flows.

Aircraft

Manage Profiles: Opens the Aircraft Profiles screen where you can add, edit, rename, or delete saved aircraft. Each profile stores your aircraft's type, tail number, cruise speed, fuel capacity, and other performance data used for flight planning and ownship identification.

Downloads

Download Navigator: Opens the Regional Data Manager for syncing charts, terrain, and navigation data from your local network server. This is where you download the offline data required before every flight.

12. Troubleshooting

No Aircraft Symbol / No Movement on the Map

  • Confirm your tablet is on the same network as SkyView (or its relay).
  • Open Settings → Debug and enable Network (UDP Relay) — it shows whether this app instance is the relay (PRIMARY) or a follower, and the current packet rate per port. Zero packets means no data is arriving on that network.
  • Enable Dynon Datastreams to see the raw parsed ADAHRS/GPS/system values as they update.

Charts, Terrain, or Airspace Not Loading

  • Go to Data Management and check the server status line. Server: Unreachable means your tablet can't find the local data server via mDNS — confirm it's running and on the same network.
  • Check whether the relevant region/data-type cell shows Not cached — it needs to be synced at least once before it will render.

Weather/NOTAM Panel Showing Red Status Line

This means the ground (Wi-Fi) fetch is currently failing; GreatEFB retries every 30 seconds automatically. You may still see data if you're receiving FIS-B over the air.

NOTAM Section Shows "Unable to Check NOTAMs"

This is a deliberate safety message, not an error to dismiss — it means the app could not confirm the absence of NOTAMs for that airport, not that there are none.

No Connection Indicator for SkyView

Known current limitation — there is no dedicated connection-status indicator yet. Use the Debug overlays above as the practical substitute.
